What the Solari run
actually proved.

Solari Browser researched product choices for an eight-item SmartCart list. Solari Sandbox compared complete baskets and made one useful tradeoff:

Chosen basket $24.20
instead of
Cheapest basket $23.57

Spend $0.63 more to avoid buying about 1.5 lb of extra chicken.

That is the value of the Sandbox step: it did not blindly choose the lowest total. It found a complete basket that stayed within SmartCart’s $0.75 price limit while reducing unnecessary food.

In plain English

Research, compare,
check the answer.

  1. 01
    Solari Browser found the choices.

    It opened 16 approved Demo Grocer product pages for eight shopping needs and recorded each product, package size, visible demo price, source, and time.

  2. 02
    Solari Sandbox compared whole baskets.

    It checked package coverage, total cost, and leftover quantity together—not one ingredient at a time.

  3. 03
    SmartCart checked the result.

    SmartCart’s verification layer checked that every ingredient was covered and that the recommendation stayed inside the allowed price difference.

Honest boundaries

Real Solari execution.
Controlled retailer data.

This run used SmartCart’s owned Demo Grocer, a synthetic retailer built to test the workflow safely. The receipt records that Browser and Sandbox completed the research and basket decision; its prices are not current Walmart, Target, or commercial-retailer prices.

  • No retailer account was accessed.
  • No cart was created or changed.
  • No purchase or checkout was automated.
  • The shopper still chooses what happens next.
